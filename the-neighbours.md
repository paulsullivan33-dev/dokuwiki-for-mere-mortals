# The Neighbours

DokuWiki is one point in a small
landscape of group-writing tools. These
are its honest neighbours, characterised
from their own documentation — this
guide's lab ran only DokuWiki, and the
comparisons below are of design, not of
benchmarks.

## BookStack — the tidy, structured cousin

BookStack organises knowledge as
shelves, books, chapters and pages, and
stores it in a database (MySQL) behind
a polished editor. If your group thinks
in volumes and wants a handsome,
opinionated structure handed to it,
BookStack gives more guidance than
DokuWiki's free-form namespaces. The
trade is the database: backup, moving
and peeking inside all go through
database tools, and "the page is a
file" becomes "the page is a row". This
series has a BookStack guide for the
full picture.

## MediaWiki — the heavyweight

MediaWiki is the software Wikipedia
runs on: database-backed, enormously
capable, with categories, templates,
talk pages and an extension ecosystem
to match. For a public, encyclopaedic,
many-thousand-page project with
dedicated maintainers, it is the
proven tool. For a club's rota and
minutes it is a lorry sent for a loaf:
installation, database care and
upgrades all cost more, every week,
than the whole of DokuWiki does.

## A folder of Markdown files — the minimalist

A shared folder of Markdown notes —
edited in a text editor or an app such
as Obsidian (this series has a guide),
synced by whatever syncs folders —
gives a group the same plain-file
virtue with no web program at all.
What it lacks is the wiki's web shape:
no accounts or permissions of its own,
no edit button for the member who does
not live in a text editor, no built-in
history page or search. It is the right
answer for two technical people and the
wrong one for twenty mixed ones.

## The hosted wikis — someone else's computer

Services in the cloud (collaborative
note-and-wiki products of many brands)
remove the server entirely: sign up,
invite the group, write. In exchange,
the group's knowledge lives under
another company's terms, pricing and
continuity, exporting is a feature
rather than a folder copy, and there is
no disk to open when something is
unclear. For a group with no one willing
to keep a small server, that trade can
be the right one; this guide exists for
the groups who have, or can borrow,
that one person.

## Where DokuWiki stands

Against each neighbour the pattern is
the same: DokuWiki gives up polish,
scale or structure in exchange for
being *inspectable* — a wiki whose
whole self fits in two folders of
readable files, installable on the
humblest hosting, and understandable
end to end by the person who tends it.
For a group notebook that must outlast
its tools, that is not the consolation
prize. It is the point.
